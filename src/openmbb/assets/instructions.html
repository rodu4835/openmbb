01 What OpenMBB is

OpenMBB is a phase-gated serial console and diagnostics app for the MBB (Main Bike Board) found on Gen2 Zero electric motorcycles. It connects to the bike's service port, reads everything the bike will tell you, saves it all to disk, and interprets it offline.

The whole design is built around one idea: reads are the point; writes are the exception. You can do a complete, useful session and never change a single thing on the bike. The app is phase-gated — each phase unlocks the next, and you can stop after any of them. Closing the window never loses data; everything is written to a session folder as you go.

0 · Connect 1 · Read (baseline) 2 · Login (read-only) 3 · Writes (whitelist-only) Analyze (always available)

Which bikes

Verified: 2017 Zero FXS (MBB firmware rev 41).

Should work (same console, unverified) — Gen2 MBB Zeros, roughly 2013–2019: S, SR, DS, DSR, FX, FXS, FXE. The write panel auto-adapts: it only shows whitelisted settings that actually appear in that bike's live dump.

Not compatible as-is — pre-2013 Gen1 (9600 baud) and 2020+ Cypher III / SR-F generation. Those use a different baud and a different console language, so OpenMBB is a Gen2 tool by design.

Menu tour Everything below is also available inside the app. Help → Instructions (or press F1) reprints the full flow, Help → Wiring diagram shows the pinout, and Help → Safety notes lists exactly what the app will and won't send.

02 What you need

One cable, and a way to power the console. That's it — the app bundles Python, Tk, and every dependency into a single file (or install from source with pip install .).

The cable: FTDI TTL-232R-3V3

A 3.3 V TTL USB-to-serial cable, wired to the OBD-II / C3 service port (on 2017+ FX/FXS it's under the seat). Serial is 38400 8-N-1, no flow control, CR-LF newlines.

FTDI wireOBD pinRole
Black (GND)5Diagnostic ground
Yellow (RXD)8bike MBB Tx
Orange (TXD)9bike MBB Rx
Red (+5 V)—NEVER connected
Full pinout lives in the app For the authoritative wiring, open Help → Wiring diagram. Before you connect, confirm the FTDI Orange line idles around 3.3 V versus Black, and that the Red lead is taped off.

Powering the console

The easy way You do not have to key the bike on to read it — plugging in the AC charger wakes the MBB and the console is fully live for reads (the bike shows Mode: Charging). One caveat: isolation-resistance readings are only valid OFF the charger (the onboard charger ties the HV bus to mains earth and makes the isolation monitor read falsely low), so OpenMBB flags any isolation reading taken while charging.
No bike? No problem Pick SIMULATOR (no bike) in the Port dropdown and click through the entire tool at your desk — connect, read, log in, even the Writes flow, all against synthetic fixtures. It's the best way to learn the app before you ever touch the motorcycle.

03 The guided flow

Four tabs, in order, each one unlocking the next. The blue (cyan) buttons are the primary action at each step. You can stop after any phase and take the session folder home.

0
Phase 0 · Connect tab

Test your cable, then connect

Step 1 — 1 · Verify link. This does exactly one thing: it opens the port and only listens — it transmits nothing in software. So it safely proves your cable, baud, and that the bike is talking, with zero risk on any cable. Power the bike during the ~45 second listen window (key ON, or just plug in the AC charger) to catch the boot banner.

Step 2 — 2 · Connect & Probe. This wakes the ZERO MBB> prompt (retried a few times), rejects wrong-baud / Tx-Rx-swapped garbage instead of trusting a stray >, and requires the version banner to actually parse before it unlocks Phase 1.

Garbage at 38400 baud almost always means the Tx/Rx wires are swapped — stop and recheck. A firmware rev other than 41 still connects, but you'll get a warning before any write.

1
Phase 1 · Read tab

Read the bike, then capture a baseline

Every button here is a one-shot read — bms, status, stats, sevcon, and so on (hover any button for a plain-language tooltip). Each grabs a snapshot and saves it to the session folder.

The primary action is the blue ★ FULL BASELINE button. It runs the quick reads plus the full settings dump (your backup) plus the small errorlogdump. Individual reads do not unlock Login — only FULL BASELINE does, so a backup always exists before anything can change.

A "Watch" checkbox can repeat one light read on a timer (reads only — great for a charge session). There's also a raw-command box, but it's blocklist-enforced and read-only in intent.

You can stop right here — reads are the whole point of day one.

2
Phase 2 · Analyze tab (no bike needed)

Make sense of it in Analyze

Analyze is always available — it reads a saved session folder (or the current one) and interprets it, flagging everything ok / watch / alert:

Health — SOC vs pack voltage, cell balance, capacity, temps, charge cycles, and the effective gearing ratio.  Rides — per-ride distance, SOC%/km and temps from a ride log you load (a decoded zero-log-parser export; rev 41 doesn't stream ride telemetry as console text).  Compare — pick 2+ sessions to track settings changes and capacity / gearing trends over time.  Gearing — enter new front/rear teeth to get the ratio and the exact spfront / sprear / rwhcirc values to write.

The parsers are deliberately tolerant: a field the capture didn't include shows n/a rather than failing.

3
Phase 3 · Login tab · optional

Log in — read-only, just reveals the tunables

Logging in changes nothing on the bike. On rev 41 the tuning settings (spfront, sprear, regen, …) are login-gated — set at level 0 shows identity only. Logging in simply reveals those hidden settings and unlocks the Writes tab.

Click Try known passwords to attempt the community-known ones in order, or type your own and Try this password. A typed password is masked in every log and never written to disk unless you say yes when it offers to remember it. A failed attempt is totally fine — you just stay read-only.

Success is confirmed by the read-only login level query (Login Level: N), not by guessing at the reply wording. The post-login set is saved as the authoritative pre-change baseline.

4
Phase 4 · Writes tab · triple-gated

Writes — whitelist-only, backed up, verified, revertible

Writes are triple-gated: you must be logged in and arm the master UNLOCK WRITES toggle and confirm each individual write. Rows appear only for settings that are both on the whitelist and present in your bike's live dump. Change one thing at a time.

Every write is journaled with a per-entry Revert button. If the read-back doesn't match, the app offers to stage a revert to the previous value immediately.

Where it all saves Everything lands in ~/Documents/OpenMBB/openmbb-sessions/<timestamp>_<port>/ — the raw byte log, one file per command, the settings baselines/backups, and the writes journal. Change the location via Session → Set save location…; the current target is shown (and clickable) in the top status strip.

04 The safety rules

OpenMBB's safety model is enforced in the transport layer — not just the UI. That means no button, no dialog, and not even the raw-command box can send a blocked command.

Do this every session

  • Work with the bike PARKED on its stand, key on, kill switch OFF. Console work is done stationary.
  • Never stream the console while riding. Full stop.
  • Run FULL BASELINE before anything else — a settings backup must exist before any change is possible.
  • No writes on day one. Bring the session folder home, review it in Analyze, then decide.

Read-first, whitelist-only writes

The transport refuses dangerous commands from every path — including a hard blocklist of destructive commands (EEPROM erase/format, settingsrst, statsrst, log clears/adds, reset, exit_to_bl, dtc_clear, force_all_storage_mode, burn, and more) plus any set of a protected value (thermal/regen guards, abs_disable, bypass_bms, identity like model/VIN/serial).

No smuggling, no back-door writes. The transport rejects any command containing a control character (so a pasted second line can't slip onto the wire), and all set <name> <value> writes are refused from the raw box — writes go only through the gated Writes-tab flow. A typed password is masked in everything written to disk for the whole session.

⚠ The heavy log dumps can drop the drivetrain contactor The full-log reads — eventlogdump and dumpall (~1 MB, several minutes at 38400 baud) — are deliberately kept out of the routine baseline and sit behind their own buttons with a confirm dialog. On a keyed-on bike a long dump can starve the MBB's CAN servicing enough that the BMS briefly opens the drivetrain contactor — you'll hear a click and see a flashing dash; it recovers when the read finishes. The bike must be safely parked. This is observed-live behavior, not theoretical.
Guards you can see but not change Regen and thermal guards are shown read-only in the Writes tab (and under Bike → Write options). Validators catch foot-guns too: a coast regen of exactly 0 is refused (fishtail risk), and there are range limits on every numeric. A mid-session reboot or a silent console is detected and surfaced — login state is re-locked rather than trusted.

05 Short FAQ

Do I have to change anything on my bike to use this?

No. OpenMBB is read-first by design. You can connect, run FULL BASELINE, and analyze the results without ever logging in or writing a single value. Writing is optional, off by default, and triple-gated.

Does logging in change my bike or void anything on its own?

Logging in is read-only. It only reveals the tuning settings the console hides at level 0 and unlocks the Writes tab — it changes nothing on the bike. A failed attempt just leaves you read-only. (Actually changing a setting is a separate, gated action, and changing settings may affect your warranty — you're responsible for what you write.)

My connect gives garbage or "no prompt". What now?

Run 1 · Verify link first — it listens only and proves your RX wiring and baud. If you see banner signatures, the link is good. If you get non-text garbage at 38400 baud, the Tx/Rx wires are almost certainly swapped — stop and recheck the pinout (Help → Wiring diagram). If nothing arrives at all, make sure the bike is powered during the listen window (key ON or AC charger plugged in) and check GND to pin 5.

Should I run eventlogdump / dumpall right away?

No — they're intentionally not in the baseline. Those full-log dumps can briefly open the drivetrain contactor on a keyed-on bike, so they're gated behind their own confirm dialog. Do them only when you specifically need the full event log, with the bike safely parked.

What password does it use to log in?

The Try known passwords button tries community-reported passwords in order. You can also type your own. Anything you type is masked in the logs and never saved to disk unless you explicitly choose to remember it after a successful login (clear saved ones via Session → Forget saved login passwords).

My firmware isn't rev 41 — can I still use it?

Yes for reads. A different rev still connects, but you'll get a warning because OpenMBB's safety lists, parsers, and write whitelist were verified against rev 41 only. Reads are fine; be cautious about writes. The write panel also auto-adapts, only showing settings that actually appear in your bike's live dump.

Where does my data go, and is it safe to share?

Sessions save under ~/Documents/OpenMBB/openmbb-sessions/ (changeable in the Session menu). Every byte is timestamped in session_raw.log, with one file per command plus the settings baselines/backups and the writes journal. Passwords are masked in everything on disk. The exportable health report (Session → Save health report) deliberately omits VIN/serial so a shared report leaks no identifiers.

Can I learn the app without a motorcycle?

Yes — pick SIMULATOR (no bike) in the Port dropdown (or launch with --sim). It walks through every phase against synthetic fixtures so you can rehearse the whole flow safely at your desk.
